Reading the values

Bar length
By default each assay's bars are scaled to the gene's own range: RNA from zero to the highest line, on a log₁₀(TPM + 1) scale; protein from the lowest to the highest quantified line, on the log₂ scale. Shared axis keeps each assay's range fixed across genes. Protein baselines are not calibrated for comparing absolute abundance between proteins. Printed values keep the supplied units.
RNA and protein
TPM (within-sample normalised transcript abundance) and TMT log₂ protein abundance are different assays in different units. Compare them by rank and pattern, not by matching numbers. Protein differences can reflect post-transcriptional biology as well as TMT measurement effects.
n.d.
No quantified value in that assay. About 9% of protein values are missing, in blocks: a protein absent from a TMT plex is missing for every line in that plex, not necessarily absent from the cells. Absence of protein is weaker evidence than presence.
Histotype, site and plex
Histotype, source site and TMT plex are partly confounded, and several histotype-by-centre combinations are not represented. A difference that tracks subtype may partly reflect batch or site. Models from the same patient are not independent biological replicates.
Gene identifiers
RNA is keyed by Ensembl gene ID (release 93) and mapped to symbols. Where several Ensembl genes share a symbol, their TPM values are summed and every contributing ID is listed under the gene name. Protein rows use a representative symbol, or a symbol plus UniProt identifier for secondary features. Unmatched features and unmeasured assays are labelled.
Replicates
Each line has one library or replicate per assay, so values are single measurements, rounded for display. ‡ marks a line with a subtype label conflict, described in the resource notes.
